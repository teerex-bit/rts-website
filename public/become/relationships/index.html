Release Control — Whole Surrender to God and His Will

You cannot fully follow God while reserving the right to decide where He may lead you.

We often want God to take control of what we cannot control while keeping control of everything we think we can. That is not yet whole surrender.

A person can pray sincerely, listen for God’s direction, seek His help, and still approach Him with an unspoken boundary:

“

“Show me what You want—and then I will decide whether I agree.”

Listening deeply eventually brings us to the question: What happens when God’s will and mine are not the same?

JESUS GIVES US THE CLEAREST PICTURE

In Gethsemane, Jesus does not pretend not to have a preference:

“Yet not as I will, but as you will.” — Matthew 26:39

Surrender does not require having no will. Jesus had a will. Surrender meant His will was placed beneath the Father’s will.

Whole surrender isn’t the absence of desire.

It is the surrender of desire’s authority.

WHAT ARE WE ACTUALLY SURRENDERING?

We surrender more than outcomes.

  • My need to understand before obeying.
  • My need to know what happens next.
  • My preferred timing.
  • My preferred outcome.
  • My right to protect the version of myself I have constructed.
  • My demand that God accomplish His purposes in ways that make sense to me.
  • And ultimately: my claim to be the final authority over my own life.

A NECESSARY DISTINCTION

Control says:

“I need to know enough to make myself safe.”

Surrender says:

“I need to know the One I am trusting.”

Surrender is only reasonable if God is trustworthy. If God is unpredictable, controlling, manipulative, withholding, or fundamentally against my good, surrender would be terrifying.

But if Jesus really shows us the Father—if God really is good, really loves us, really knows what we cannot see—then surrender stops being resignation.

Surrender becomes the reasonable response to trust.
