REFORMING THE SOUL

AN INVITATION TO LIFE WITH GOD

A Different Way of Living With God

Many of us have spent years learning what God wants us to do. But somewhere along the way, we may have missed what it means to live with Him.

Reforming the Soul is an invitation to explore that possibility.

Throughout Scripture, we discover a God who desires more than our obedience, service, or religious devotion. He desires relationship. From the beginning, humanity was created for life in His presence—not merely to believe in Him, but to walk with Him.

And that relationship was never intended to be separate from ordinary life.

This journey is about recovering that way of living. It is not another set of things to do for God. It is an invitation to discover what begins to change in us when we share our lives with Him.

Along the way, we will learn to recognize what has shaped us, see ourselves and God more clearly, live with Him in the present, and participate in what He is doing in the world around us.

There is no spiritual performance to achieve here, and no ideal version of yourself you must become before beginning.

The invitation is simply to discover what life with God can actually become.

The Person We've Become

Long before we gave much thought to the kind of people we were becoming, life was shaping us. Our families, friendships, successes, failures, disappointments and experiences with God all left something behind.

Much of that formation happened without our awareness.

Some of us learned to keep the peace by not saying what we really felt. Others learned to be the responsible one, the one everyone could count on. We learned when to speak, when to stay quiet, whom we could trust and what we needed to do to feel accepted. We were learning how to get through life without necessarily knowing that we were learning.

Eventually, these responses became so familiar that they simply felt like us.

Then we came to God carrying all of it.

We may have learned what Christians believe and how Christians are supposed to behave. We may have learned Scripture, attended church, prayed, served, worshiped, and genuinely desired to follow Jesus. Yet underneath all of that, the person who had been formed through years of living was still there.

That is where Reforming the Soul begins.

We are not beginning by asking you to try harder to be a better Christian. We are beginning by noticing the person you bring into your relationship with God.

A MOMENT TO NOTICE

What has shaped you?

A word, a memory, or even a familiar feeling may come to mind as you read these. Explore whichever feels familiar to your own story.

Perhaps you learned early that mistakes were something to hide, or that it was safer to keep your feelings to yourself. Those lessons can follow us long after we leave home.

When people we trusted were there for us, we learned something about closeness. When they weren't, we may have learned to hold something back. We can carry either experience into relationships with people who had nothing to do with it.

Some of us feel good about ourselves only when we are accomplishing something. Others are reluctant to try because they remember what happened the last time they failed. Those experiences may have shaped what we expect, but they do not have to define us.

Pain can leave us expecting another loss, protecting ourselves before anything has happened. Sometimes a response that once helped us endure a difficult season continues long after that season has passed.

We may have learned to be whatever someone needed us to be so that we would feel wanted. After a while, pleasing people or hiding how we really feel may seem like just the way we are.

Not every formative experience fits a category. A particular season, responsibility, change, or unexpected moment may have influenced the way you see yourself, others, or God.

AWAKEN

We Begin by Waking Up

We begin by noticing things about ourselves that we may have lived with for years without really seeing.

Imagine someone says something that makes you feel dismissed. Before you have had time to think about it, something happens inside you. Maybe you become defensive. Maybe you withdraw. Maybe you feel an immediate need to explain yourself. Perhaps you become angry or spend the rest of the day replaying the conversation.

Normally, we focus on the other person.

They shouldn't have said that.

Awaken asks us to notice something else.

What just happened in me?

That is a very different question.

We are not immediately deciding whether our response was right or wrong. We are not condemning ourselves for having it. We are simply learning to pay attention.

Why did that bother me so much? Why did I feel threatened? Why was it so important that I be understood? Why do I respond this way whenever someone questions me? Why does this particular situation keep producing the same reaction?

Sometimes the first thing we notice is the conversation already going on within us: “They don't respect me,” “I need to explain,” or “I can't let this happen again.” We may also notice our shoulders tighten or our voice change before we have decided what to say. These are small opportunities to become aware of what is happening within us while the moment is still unfolding.

At first, we may recognize these things long after they happen. Then we begin catching them sooner. Eventually, we sometimes recognize them while they are happening.

We are beginning to see that there is more going on within us than the words we say or the things we do.

Some of what we call our personality may be the ways we learned to protect ourselves. We may have learned to stay quiet to avoid conflict, or to explain ourselves before anyone could misunderstand us. Eventually those responses became so familiar that we hardly noticed them.

Those ways of protecting ourselves may have helped us get through something difficult. We don't need to despise the person we became. We can begin bringing those fears and habits to God, learning to receive from Him the security we have been trying to provide for ourselves.

We are learning to catch ourselves being ourselves.

And eventually we can say, “Something is happening in me, and I can see it.”

That is awakening.

But seeing what is happening does not necessarily tell us why it is happening. For that, we have to look deeper.

JAMES 1:19–20 · WEB
“So, then, my beloved brothers, let every man be swift to hear, slow to speak, and slow to anger; for the anger of man doesn't produce the righteousness of God.”

There is something about slowing down that gives us a chance to hear what is being said, both around us and within us. We can begin to notice what is rising in us before it becomes our response.

AWAKEN · PRACTICING ATTENTION

Notice Without Needing to Explain

After a difficult conversation, we may spend hours thinking about what the other person said. We replay it, imagine what we should have said, and wonder why they acted that way. There is another part of that conversation we can begin to notice: what was happening within us.

Noticing does not mean picking ourselves apart. We can admit that we felt hurt, defensive or afraid without deciding whether we were right to feel that way. We can let God into what we are seeing before we know what to do about it.

PSALM 139:23–24 · WEB
“Search me, God, and know my heart. Try me, and know my thoughts. See if there is any wicked way in me, and lead me in the everlasting way.”

David is asking God to show him what he cannot see for himself. We can come to God in the same way, without having to figure ourselves out first.

Bring an ordinary moment into view

Recall a recent moment that stayed with you. Something interrupted your plans, disappointed you, pleased you, or brought a stronger reaction than you expected.

Notice what happened and how you responded. There is no need to explain or judge your reaction.

For example: “Someone corrected me, and I immediately began explaining myself. I noticed tension and a strong need to be understood.”

What happened, and what did you notice?

Take a moment to consider this quietly with God, then continue when you are ready.

“Lord, here is what I noticed. I do not need to understand it all before I bring it to You.”

PART I · SEE YOURSELF CLEARLY

Then We Learn to See Clearly

Someone questions a decision you have made, and suddenly the conversation is no longer about the decision. You feel the need to establish that you knew what you were doing, to explain why you made the choice, or to defend your ability to make it. Somewhere in the exchange, what you hear becomes more significant than what was actually said.

Perhaps being questioned has come to mean being doubted. Perhaps being wrong has become associated with losing the respect of someone whose opinion matters to you. Those associations can become so deeply ingrained that we experience them as reality rather than as conclusions we have drawn from our experiences.

We respond not only to what is happening, but to what we have come to believe it means.

Awaken brings those responses into view. See Clearly invites us to look at the understanding of life from which they come. We begin to discover how much of what feels true to us has been shaped by the life we have already lived.

ROMANS 12:2 · WEB
“Don’t be conformed to this world, but be transformed by the renewing of your mind, so that you may prove what is the good, well-pleasing, and perfect will of God.”

The renewing of our minds involves more than acquiring new information about God. It reaches into the convictions we have been living from, some of which we may never have put into words. God is not asking us to deny what we have experienced; He is inviting us to see it in the light of what is true.

Over time, the ways we learned to navigate life can become part of how we understand ourselves. The person who learned to anticipate rejection may come to believe they are someone people inevitably leave. The person who learned to establish their worth through achievement may have difficulty imagining they could be loved apart from what they accomplish.

There is a distinction between the person we have been formed to be and the life we have been given in Christ. We can acknowledge the history behind our responses without allowing that history to determine our identity. As we begin to see ourselves and God more clearly, we discover that the things we have long believed about ourselves do not have to govern the life we share with Him.

PART I · SEE YOURSELF CLEARLY · THE INNER PATH

What We See Shapes What We Become

When something happens, we don't always see it the same way someone else does. What we see affects what we believe, what we expect, what we want and eventually what we choose to do. Much of this happens so quickly that we may never notice it.

Something happens, and I give it a meaning. I may see a correction as an insult, while someone else sees it as help.

The meaning I give it fits into something I already believe about myself, others or God.

Because I believe it, I begin expecting something similar to happen again.

I begin wanting something: to feel safe, to be accepted, to stay in control or to avoid being hurt.

I start deciding how I will get what I want or keep the thing I fear from happening.

That decision begins to show up in what I say, do or hold back.

As I keep making similar choices, they become familiar. Eventually this is how I live without having to think much about it.

PROVERBS 4:23 · WEB
“Keep your heart with all diligence, for out of it is the wellspring of life.”

Scripture speaks of the heart as more than our feelings. It is where our desires, beliefs and choices come from. What other people see in our response may have been taking shape within us long before they saw it.

This helps explain why we can keep making the same choices even when we know better. There is something underneath those choices worth bringing into the light.

Maybe what appears to be anger is actually self-protection. Perhaps what looks like ambition is being fueled by a need for approval. What looks like independence may actually reveal how difficult it is for us to trust someone else.

We may learn to keep our irritation to ourselves and still feel just as irritated. Something within us has not yet changed.

When a belief becomes an identity

We do not only form opinions about events. Over time we also form a story about ourselves: I am the responsible one. I am the one people leave. I have to be useful to be wanted. I must never make a mistake. A story repeated often enough can begin to feel like a fact, even when it was first learned in a particular season of life.

If I believe I have to be right to be accepted, a correction feels threatening. If I believe I have to be needed to be loved, someone else's growing independence may feel like rejection. What looks to someone else like a small thing may touch something in me that has been there for years.

EPHESIANS 2:8–10 · WEB
“For by grace you have been saved through faith, and that not of yourselves; it is the gift of God, not of works, that no one would boast. For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works”

Paul tells us that we are God's workmanship before he talks about the good things we will do. We don't have to earn our place with God by getting everything right. We can learn to live from the place He has already given us in Christ.

Guilt, shame, and the invitation to return

There is a difference between saying, “I did something wrong,” and believing, “There is something wrong with me.” The first can lead us to admit what happened and make it right. The second can leave us hiding, even from God. We can take responsibility for what we did without letting it tell us who we are.

After Adam and Eve disobeyed God, they hid from Him. We can recognize something of ourselves in that story. When we fail, do we find ourselves wanting to stay away from the very One who can meet us in it?

Look Beneath Your Response

Look beneath an ordinary moment

Recall a recent situation that brought a reaction you would like to understand.

What did you think was happening? Did you hear criticism as rejection, or an interruption as someone taking advantage of you? You don't have to find a hidden motive. Simply notice what you understood the moment to mean.

How This Happens in Everyday Life

Let’s look at how an ordinary situation can reveal what is happening beneath our response.

When Someone Criticizes You

Someone points out a problem with work I put effort into.

They think I am not good enough.

Nothing I do will satisfy them.

I want to feel accepted and appreciated.

I will protect myself from feeling inadequate.

I defend myself or dismiss what they say.

If repeated, I become defensive even when someone is trying to help.

What might I not be seeing? The criticism may be intended to help, rather than to judge my worth.

When Your Plans Are Interrupted

My plans are unexpectedly interrupted by someone’s request.

They do not respect my time.

People will keep taking advantage of me.

I want to stay in control of my day.

I will protect my plans from interference.

I respond with irritation or refuse to help.

If repeated, I become less available to others and more controlling.

What might I not be seeing? The interruption may be an opportunity to care for someone, or simply an inconvenience that does not require me to defend myself.

What was I trying to protect, and what might God be inviting me to see differently?

What were you taking to be true?

Take a moment to consider this quietly with God, then continue when you are ready.

2 CORINTHIANS 10:5 · WEB
“bringing every thought into captivity to the obedience of Christ”

Paul is talking about things that set themselves against the knowledge of God. We don't need to examine every passing thought, but we can bring the things we have come to believe before Jesus and let Him help us see what is true.

We can know that something happened without knowing what it meant. Sometimes those two things get so closely tied together that we cannot tell them apart.

Imagine sending a message to a friend and receiving no reply. The fact is that you have not heard back. The conclusion may be that you no longer matter to them. That conclusion can feel entirely true, but it is not the only possible explanation. We can bring both the experience and our interpretation before God without pretending either that the hurt is unreal or that our conclusion is certain.

Independence can look like strength

Being capable and dependable can be a good thing. But some of us have become so accustomed to doing everything ourselves that receiving help feels like weakness. We can even approach God that way, asking Him to bless a decision we have already made instead of listening to Him as we make it.

JOHN 15:4–5 · WEB
“Remain in me, and I in you. As the branch can’t bear fruit by itself unless it remains in the vine, so neither can you, unless you remain in me.”

A branch doesn't have to work out how to produce its own life. It receives life from the vine. Jesus is inviting us into that kind of relationship with Him, not teaching us how to become so capable that we no longer need Him.

As we notice what we are trying to protect, another question begins to emerge. Have I been trying to provide for myself something I could be receiving from God?

SEE GOD CLEARLY

We have begun examining the person we have become. Now we turn toward the One we are learning to trust. What we believe about God shapes what we expect from Him, and those expectations influence how we live with Him.

What Do I Actually Believe About God?

When life doesn't go the way we hoped, we may discover something about the God we actually believe in. We may know He is good and still wonder whether He has forgotten us.

When I disappoint Him, do I expect Him to pull away? When something goes wrong, do I wonder what I did to deserve it? Do I feel free to come to Him, or do I need to get myself together first?

Can I trust Him when I don't understand what is happening? Or do I feel that I need to take care of things myself?

What we say about God and what we expect from Him may not be the same.

These questions are not about whether we can give the right answers. They help us notice what we actually expect from God.

JOHN 14:9 · WEB
“He who has seen me has seen the Father.”

Jesus is telling us that when we look at Him, we are seeing the Father. If the God we have imagined is different from the One we see in Jesus, we have somewhere to begin again.

We might say that God loves us and still find ourselves avoiding Him after a bad day. We might believe He is in control and quietly fear what He will do if we let go. What we know to say and what we have learned to expect may be two different things.

How our picture of God is formed

If a parent was hard to please, we may find ourselves expecting God to be hard to please. If someone we trusted was unpredictable, it may be difficult to trust Him when we cannot see what is coming. Those experiences matter, but they do not have to tell us what God is like.

It's difficult to surrender to someone we believe might hurt us. It's difficult to receive love when we believe we will owe something in return. These beliefs can shape our relationship with God in ways we have never noticed.

LUKE 15:20 · WEB
“But while he was still far off, his father saw him, and was moved with compassion, and ran, and fell on his neck, and kissed him.”

The son had prepared a speech before coming home. But his father ran to him before he could make things right. His choices still mattered; what he discovered was that he was already wanted.

PART II · SEE GOD CLEARLY · JESUS AS OUR LENS

Learning to See God as He Is

When we are unsure what God is like, Jesus gives us someone to look at.

Perhaps we have learned to think of God as distant, disappointed or difficult to please. We can bring that understanding to Jesus and notice how He actually receives people.

Watch how He speaks to someone who has failed, how He notices someone others overlook, and how He responds to someone who is afraid. What does that tell us about the Father?

Jesus is not the exception to what God is like. He is the revelation of what God is like.

As we come to know Him, we may begin to find that we don't need to earn the love He is giving us. We can risk trusting Him with things we have always tried to handle ourselves.

HEBREWS 1:3 · WEB
“His Son is the radiance of his glory, the very image of his substance”

Jesus does not ignore sin or pretend that our choices don't matter. And yet people who have failed are able to come near Him. We don't have to imagine that Jesus is kinder than the Father who sent Him.

There will still be things we do not understand. But when our circumstances leave us wondering what God is like, we can return to Jesus.

Jesus reveals more than a reassuring idea

When Jesus touches someone others will not touch, eats with people others reject, or speaks truth to someone who has failed, He is showing us the Father. His love does not require us to pretend nothing needs to change. It means we can come to Him as we are.

LUKE 10:22 · WEB
“No one knows who the Son is, except the Father, and who the Father is, except the Son, and he to whomever the Son desires to reveal him.”

We may have lived with a picture of God that came from someone else's treatment of us. We can begin allowing what we see in Jesus to change what we expect from Him.

In John 14–17, Jesus does more than describe His life with the Father. Through the Spirit, He invites us into that life. The relationship we see in Jesus is not something we can only admire from a distance.

Trust does not require an explanation for everything

We can tell God that we are disappointed, that we don't understand, or that we are tired of waiting. We don't have to explain away our pain or find an answer before we can remain with Him.

Bring your understanding to Jesus

Think of an expectation you carry about yourself or God, especially when something is difficult.

Bring that expectation to Jesus. Does what you see in Him change what you have been expecting? If you are still unsure, you can tell Him that too.

PART II · SEE GOD CLEARLY · WHAT IS TRUE

When Knowing Becomes Trust

Awaken made it possible to say, “I know what I did.” See Clearly invites us to go further: “I am starting to understand where that came from, and I am learning what is actually true.”

We may begin to recognize that behind our anger was fear, or that our need to control came from a fear of being left alone. But understanding why we feel something is only part of what is possible.

We can begin to bring those very fears into our life with God, instead of letting them decide how we relate to Him.

Trust precedes understanding.

Knowing what is true and learning to live from it are not always the same thing.

From an inherited picture to a living relationship

I can believe that God is good and still be afraid to trust Him with something that matters to me. I can know I am accepted in Christ and still want to hide when I fail. Learning to live with Him means discovering what those truths mean in the very places where I struggle to believe them.

EPHESIANS 1:17–18 · WEB
“…that the God of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of glory, may give to you a spirit of wisdom and revelation in the knowledge of him, having the eyes of your hearts enlightened, that you may know what is the hope of his calling…”

Paul is praying for people who already believe. He wants them to see what God has given them. We may know we are loved and still be learning what it is to live as someone who is loved.

Suppose you are waiting for news that matters deeply to you. Trust may begin simply by telling God what you hope for, admitting what you fear, and choosing to remain with Him while you wait. You do not have to know what He will do before you can entrust yourself to Him.

BECOME · LIVING WITH GOD

Learning to Live From Trust

We may understand something clearly while we are sitting quietly with God, only to find ourselves reacting in an old familiar way a few hours later. That is not a failure of the journey. Those ordinary moments are where we begin learning to live what we have come to see.

Look at the life of Jesus. He was continually living with the Father, paying attention to Him and responding to the people and circumstances before Him. We can learn to live with God in our own ordinary moments in the same way.

JOHN 5:19 · WEB
“Most certainly, I tell you, the Son can do nothing of himself, but what he sees the Father doing. For whatever things he does, these the Son also does likewise.”

Jesus was not doing the Father's work apart from the Father. The life He invites us into is one we live with God, not one we work out on our own.

When something happens, we can begin returning to a simple way of being with Him. We release our demand to control it, receive the moment, take the next right step, and do it again when we need to.

These are not four tasks to perform perfectly. They are a way of returning to relationship with God in the life we are actually living.

It begins with something we can easily overlook: God is already here with us, and we can make room to listen to Him.

BECOME · PRESENCE

Recognizing His Presence

Have you ever finished praying, gotten up, and gone about your day as though your time with God was over? We may not realize how easily we separate being with Him from the rest of our lives.

We can pray in a quiet room, but we can also be with God while making breakfast, driving to work, or listening to someone tell us about their day. We don't have to stop living our ordinary lives to share them with Him.

JOHN 14:23 · WEB
“If a man loves me, he will keep my word. My Father will love him, and we will come to him, and make our home with him.”

Jesus speaks of God making His home with us. We don't have to create His presence or feel something unusual to know that He is here.

Imagine standing at the kitchen sink with a mind full of unfinished responsibilities. Nothing about the moment has to change. You can turn your attention toward God and say, “You are here with me, even in this.” Then continue with what is before you, aware that you are not doing it alone.

We'll get distracted and forget, and then remember again. There is nothing to make up for. We can simply return our attention to Him, right where we are.

BECOME · LISTENING

Learning to Listen

We may be very familiar with talking to God. We tell Him what happened, what we need, and what we hope He will do. We may be less accustomed to leaving room for Him to speak.

Perhaps a conversation has left us frustrated. Rather than immediately deciding what to say or how to fix it, we can bring the experience to God. “Father, what am I not seeing here?” We can allow a little space for Him to bring something to our attention, without demanding an immediate answer.

JOHN 14:26 · WEB
“But the Counselor, the Holy Spirit, whom the Father will send in my name, will teach you all things, and will remind you of all that I said to you.”

Jesus told His disciples that the Spirit would teach them and remind them of what He had said. We are learning to make room for that in the middle of our lives.

A passage of Scripture may come to mind, or we may begin to see the other person differently. We might notice something in ourselves we had been missing. Not every thought we have is from God. We can take time with what we hear, consider it in light of Scripture and Jesus, and talk with someone wise when the decision matters.

Sometimes nothing becomes clear. We don't need to make something up or feel that we have failed to hear Him. We can stay with Him and take whatever next step we already know to be right.

BECOME · RELEASE CONTROL

Release Control.

Release control

I release my demand that this situation must turn out the way I want before I can be okay.

We can see that we are trying to control something and still feel unable to let it go. We may even tell God we trust Him while quietly insisting that things have to work out a certain way.

We can tell God what we want, and we can do the things that are ours to do. Surrender means we are no longer insisting that He must give us the result we want before we can trust Him.

Sometimes control looks a lot like being responsible. We make plans, look after people and try to keep things from going wrong. But when we cannot be at peace unless everyone does what we hoped they would do, we may be carrying more than what is ours.

PROVERBS 3:5–6 · WEB
“Trust in Yahweh with all your heart, and don’t lean on your own understanding. In all your ways acknowledge him, and he will make your paths straight.”

We can still make a plan, have a difficult conversation, set a boundary or do something about a problem. We can also tell God what we fear might happen, without insisting that He work things out on our timetable.

I am responsible for my response, not for controlling the outcome.

Trust precedes understanding. We can take the next faithful step even when we cannot see how the story will unfold.

We can choose to be honest with another person, but we cannot choose how they will respond. We can do our part today without being able to guarantee tomorrow. Learning where our responsibility ends frees us to do what is ours with God.

BECOME · RECEIVE THE MOMENT

Receive the Moment.

Receive the moment

I can meet the life in front of me with God, rather than waiting for life to become what I wish it were.

We can spend an entire afternoon thinking about something that happened yesterday, or worrying about something that might happen tomorrow. Meanwhile, this is the moment in which we can actually be with God.

MATTHEW 6:34 · WEB
“Therefore don’t be anxious for tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself. Each day’s own evil is sufficient.”

Receiving the moment doesn't mean we like what is happening. It means we no longer have to wait for it to become something else before we can live with God in it.

Whatever this moment brings, it cannot keep me from living it with God.

A delay may show us how impatient we are. A disappointment may reveal how much we need to know what will happen next. A comment may remind us how much we want someone's approval. We can bring those things to God as we discover them.

The problem may still need to be solved, but there is something else happening in that moment. We are learning to be with Him in the middle of it.

That changes the question.

Instead of asking only, How do I get out of this? I can begin asking, What does faithfulness look like here?

If something is wrong, we may need to speak up, get help, leave an unsafe situation or grieve what has happened. Receiving the moment doesn't mean accepting mistreatment. It means we can face what is real with God.

We can say, “Lord, here I am. This is what I'm facing, and this is what it is bringing up in me.” Before we try to do something for Him, we can be received by Him.

We don't have to wait until life settles down to begin living with God. He is here with us now.

BECOME · FAITHFULNESS IN THE PRESENT

Take the Next Right Step. Repeat.

Then I take the next right step.

Not the next twenty steps. Not the step that guarantees the outcome I want. Not the step that removes every uncertainty.

Just the next faithful step available to me.

Once we have stopped insisting that the moment has to go our way, we have a little more room to consider what we might do next with God.

The next right step might mean listening before defending ourselves, speaking honestly without trying to manage another person's reaction, apologizing, setting a necessary boundary, or patiently completing an ordinary responsibility. It may be difficult, and it may not produce the result we hoped for. Its value lies in responding faithfully, not in guaranteeing an outcome.

We may not be able to change what has happened. But we can begin to respond differently within it.

And then I repeat the process.

That last part matters.

We may have released our need to control a conversation in the morning and find it returning that afternoon. We haven't undone what happened earlier. We have another opportunity to live that moment with God.

Over time we may notice sooner what is happening inside us. We may be able to return to God more easily, or listen a little longer before defending ourselves. What once required a great deal of effort can become more familiar.

Tomorrow will bring another conversation, disappointment, interruption or decision. We don't need to arrange our lives around practicing with God. Our lives will give us plenty of opportunities.

And when that happens, we practice again.

Here is how that can look at home, when the evening we were counting on suddenly changes.

01 · Release control.

Notice what you feel has to happen before you can be okay. Tell God what you hope for and what you fear. Let Him carry what you cannot control.

02 · Receive the moment.

Meet what is actually happening with Him, including what you are feeling.

03 · Take the next right step.

Do the next thing you know to be loving, honest and right. You don't have to solve everything first.

04 · Repeat.

When the same feeling or demand returns, return to God with it.

When someone changes your plans

You had planned a quiet evening, but your spouse tells you that people are coming over. You feel tension rise. Your first thought is, “My time doesn’t matter. Everyone assumes I’ll just go along.” You want to snap or withdraw.

Release control.

You notice the demand underneath your reaction: “The evening has to go as I planned before I can be okay.” You bring it honestly to God: “I wanted some rest, and I’m frustrated. Help me release the demand that everything must happen my way.” Your need for rest still matters; you are making room to respond without letting frustration lead.

Receive the moment.

The conversation in front of you becomes the place to be with God. You acknowledge your disappointment and listen to what your spouse actually says. Perhaps there is something about the situation you haven’t understood.

Take the next right step.

You say, “I was counting on a quiet evening, and I wish we’d talked before making plans. Tell me what you have in mind so we can work out tonight together.” You speak truthfully, listen, and make a decision. The next step might involve welcoming the guests, adjusting the plans, or agreeing on a boundary.

Repeat.

Later, while preparing for the visit, resentment rises again. You notice it and return to God. You don’t have to restart the whole evening or pretend the feeling disappeared. You release your demand again and choose how to respond to the next moment.

What begins to change: The evening may still be tiring, and your spouse may not respond as you hoped. But your frustration no longer has to decide how you speak to each other. You are learning to share the evening with God, even when it is not the evening you wanted.

BECOME · THE SLOW WORK OF FORMATION

Slowly, Something Begins to Happen

You may find yourself in a situation that once would have spoiled your whole day, and realize you are not carrying it the same way anymore. You still feel the disappointment, but it doesn't have quite the hold on you it used to.

Our relationships begin changing because we are changing.

2 CORINTHIANS 3:18 · WEB
“But we all, with unveiled face seeing the glory of the Lord as in a mirror, are transformed into the same image from glory to glory, even as from the Lord, the Spirit.”

Sometimes the change is in how we return

The same fear or urge to defend ourselves may still come up. The change may be that we notice it before we say something we regret, or that we turn toward God sooner when we do. Those are real beginnings.

When an unexpected bill arrives

You open a bill you were not expecting. Money is already stretched, and you cannot see how you will cover it. Your chest tightens, and your thoughts rush ahead: “We can’t handle this. I should have prevented it. What happens if something else goes wrong?” This is a place to practice the pattern we are learning.

Release control. You acknowledge the fear and your demand to have the whole problem solved immediately. You bring both to God: “I’m afraid, and I don’t know how we will cover this. Help me release the demand to know how everything will work out before I can take a step with You.”

Receive the moment. You look at what is actually in front of you: a bill that needs attention. The cost is real, but the worst future you are imagining has not happened. You can acknowledge what you do not know and meet this moment with God.

Take the next right step. You check the amount and due date, look at what money is available, and call to ask about payment options. You may need to talk honestly with your spouse or someone you trust. You take the action available today, even while the full solution remains unclear.

Repeat. Later that evening, worry rises again. You notice it without condemning yourself and return to God. You release the demand for certainty, receive the moment you are in, and consider whether there is another practical step to take or whether today’s work is done.

The bill may still be unpaid. What begins to change is how you carry it: you are learning to face a real responsibility with God, without letting fear decide every thought and action.

Practice with your own situation

Bring to mind something you are facing right now. You don't have to solve it here.

Tell God what you are afraid might happen and what you wish would happen instead. Then consider what is actually before you and whether there is something you can do with Him today.

For example: “I can release the demand to prove myself right. I can listen to the correction, ask a question, and acknowledge what is helpful.”

You may be able to return to the actual conversation. Or the next opportunity may come when something similar happens again.

What is one next right step you could take with God?

Take a moment to consider this quietly with God, then continue when you are ready.

If the same reaction returns, you have somewhere to begin again. Repeating the practice is part of learning this way of life.

Think of a little girl learning to cook with her mother. At first her mother does most of the work, and she gets to stir something or measure an ingredient. At eleven, she is helping prepare the meal alongside her mother. By twenty-one, she may be creating dishes her mother never could, and they are still cooking together. The mother's participation has changed as her daughter has grown, but they have not stopped sharing that experience. In our own life with God, becoming more able to live from His love does not mean becoming more independent of Him.

The change begins to reach things we didn't even know needed to change. What we want, how our bodies react, and the way we treat people can all begin to reflect the life we are sharing with God.

Over time, others may begin to experience something of that change in us, even before we have noticed it ourselves.

BECOME · FORMATION OF THE WHOLE PERSON

When the Change Reaches All of You

Have you ever known exactly how you wanted to respond to someone, only to hear yourself saying something entirely different? You wanted to be patient, yet something within you seemed to be moving in another direction. As we learn to live with God, He begins to reach into those places within us, too.

The will

We may know that we should listen to someone and still want to prove our point. The will is where we decide which direction to go. As we keep bringing those choices to God, we may find that we no longer have to fight quite so hard to choose what is good. We begin to want it.

The body

Someone questions us, and before we've decided what to say, our jaw tightens or our shoulders rise. Our bodies have learned how to respond, too. As we learn to stay with God in those moments, our bodies can begin to learn a different response. Sometimes what we need is rest, and we can learn to receive that from Him as well.

Relationships

Someone close to us may be surprised when we listen all the way through instead of interrupting. We might notice that we can apologize without needing the other person to apologize first. The change happening within us begins to make a difference in the life we share with them.

The soul

Perhaps we have spent years smiling on the outside while carrying resentment on the inside. As God begins to bring our thoughts, desires, choices and actions together, we can become less divided. That is part of what it means for our soul to be restored. We are learning to live one life with Him, including the parts we used to hide.

1 THESSALONIANS 5:23 · WEB
“May the God of peace himself sanctify you completely.”

Paul is praying for God to restore the whole person. He cares about all of us, not just the part that knows how a Christian should behave.

We don't need to work on each of these things as separate projects. They are all part of the person who is learning to live with God.
